## Pareto curve (the ship-decision artifact)

(540 male + 576 female files), measured by **stepping 25 ms hops over
the central 70 % of each file and taking the last non-null detection**

**Inside the 100 ms latency budget: only L=2 fits (50 ms).** L=5 (125 ms) and
L=10 (250 ms) are over budget. The accuracy curve is not flat between L=2
and L=10 — increasing L drops mean error 13.42 → 12.80 → 10.57 Hz, but the
**null rate climbs steeply** at higher L because the HMM has more frames to
get trapped in the unvoiced state.

### Reading

- L=2 within budget already beats both Stage 0 and Stage 1 on accuracy by
  a wide margin (13.42 vs 30.65 vs 39.39 Hz).
- L=10 hits the < 10 Hz target (10.57 Hz) in the read-out subset, but at
  250 ms latency AND 51 % female miss rate.
- Accuracy on the files Stage 2 *does* answer is excellent (82 % exact
  octave-bucket at L=2 vs Stage 0's 51 %).
- All synthetic stress passes 5/5 + 4/4 across all three lookbacks.

## Two methodology divergences worth noting

### (1) Stage 0 number is 30.65 Hz here, not 14.15 Hz from the post-tune baseline

Same code, same corpus, *different evaluation methodology*. Stage 1's harness
took a single 50 ms steady-state window from the file's middle. Stage 2
needs multi-frame input for the HMM, so the Stage 2 harness steps frames
across the central 70 % and takes the last non-null detection. The "last
non-null" of Stage 0 is whatever the trail-off region happens to detect on
that frame, which is noisier than a centered steady-state window.

This means **the apples-to-apples Stage 2 vs prior-baseline comparison
isn't trivial**. The fairer reading: Stage 2 L=2 (13.42 Hz) is comparable
to the post-tune baseline (14.15 Hz, single-window) — barely an
improvement, with a 50 ms latency cost and 33 % null rate on female files.

### (2) Null-rate accounting

The headline F mean numbers above are computed only over the files where
Stage 2 returned a non-null answer (the harness, like the existing
`real-speech-test.js`, excludes nulls from the error mean). For Stage 2
L=10, that's 284/576 female files. **The remaining 292 are voiced files
where Stage 2 said "unvoiced".**

If we counted misses as "got the question wrong" with their truth F0 as
the error magnitude, Stage 2 L=10 inclusive female mean ≈ 112 Hz, much
worse than baseline. So the < 10 Hz number is real *for the answered
subset* but doesn't represent the user-facing performance honestly.

## Structural finding — the voicing trap (the real surprise)

`_detectPitchPyinStage2` was instrumented on a missed file (`w03ae`,
truth F0 = 192 Hz, 12 frames in the central 70 %). Trace:

```
frame 0: stage0=null   stage2=null   obs-argmax=UNVOICED(-0.03)  α-argmax=UNVOICED
frame 1: stage0=null   stage2=null   obs-argmax=UNVOICED(-0.01)  α-argmax=UNVOICED
frame 2: stage0=188.8  stage2=null   obs-argmax=UNVOICED(-0.26)  α-argmax=UNVOICED
frame 3: stage0=183.9  stage2=null   obs-argmax=UNVOICED(-0.39)  α-argmax=UNVOICED
frame 4: stage0=null   stage2=null   obs-argmax=UNVOICED(-0.04)  α-argmax=UNVOICED
... (HMM stays in unvoiced state for all 12 frames)
```

Frames 2 and 3 have observation mass for voiced states (Stage 0
correctly detects ~188 Hz). But `obs[unvoiced]` is still dominant at
~exp(−0.26) = 0.77, because the deepest CMND minimum is around 0.15 in
real speech (vs. ≪ 0.01 in synthetic) and the Beta(2, 18) integration
gives that shallow minimum only ~25 % voicing mass.

Once the HMM enters the unvoiced state at frame 0 with a strongly
unvoiced observation, escape is structurally hard. With switch_prob = 0.01:

- `unvoiced → unvoiced` log-prob ≈ −0.01
- `unvoiced → voiced_s` log-prob ≈ log(0.01/300) ≈ −10.3

To switch out requires `obs[voiced_s] − obs[unvoiced] > 10.3` in log
space, which never happens for marginal-CMND voiced frames.

### Why this hits Hillenbrand and not synthetic

Synthetic harmonic stimuli have CMND ≈ 0 at the true period.
F_β(0) = 0, so voicing mass = 1.0 and obs[unvoiced] = 0. The HMM never
enters the unvoiced state. Synthetic 2nd/3rd-dom passes 5/5 + 4/4
because the algorithm is *correct on clean signals*.

Real speech has CMND ≈ 0.05–0.20 at the true period (mic noise, jitter,
spectral coloration). F_β(0.10) ≈ 0.55, so voicing mass ≈ 0.45 at most
on a typical voiced female frame. obs[unvoiced] ≈ 0.55 at the same
frame. The HMM treats voicing as marginal, picks unvoiced, and is
trapped.

### Why this is option-(A)'s known cost showing up earlier than expected

The single-unvoiced-super-state design (vs. paper's voicing-duplicated
state space, option (B)) collapses voicing into a single binary
decision per frame. When the per-frame voicing posterior is genuinely
marginal — as it is on real speech where CMND minima are shallow — the
HMM has no way to track pitch *through* the marginal frame.

In the paper's option-(B) design, every observation contributes mass to
*both* the voiced and unvoiced twin of each pitch state, so the HMM
maintains pitch context even when individual frames look unvoiced.
Decoding then reads voicing from which twin was selected, but pitch
tracking continues uninterrupted.

We deferred (B) to save 4× compute on the read that "Hillenbrand is
all-voiced, voicing-continuity won't show up". **That read was wrong:**
voicing-continuity matters even on Hillenbrand because individual
frames in real recordings *look* unvoiced even when the file is voiced.

## What I am NOT proposing to fix in this turn (per scope rule)

- Switch_prob retuning: explicitly prohibited by your "do NOT silently
  retune" rule. With switch_prob = 0.05 the trap probably softens; I
  haven't run that and I won't unless you say so.
- Observation-model rewrite: e.g., scale `obs[unvoiced]` down by a
  fixed factor, or use a different voicing decision than `1 - voicingMass`.
  Both are tuning.
- Switch from option (A) to option (B): the right fix architecturally,
  but it's a redesign of the state space, transition matrix, observation
  model, and decode rule. Multi-day, not a tweak.
- Multi-mult deletion: still in the legacy path under PYIN_STAGE=0,
  unchanged per your instruction.

## Decisions for you

The success criterion was: **F mean < 10 Hz on real-speech-test.js at
some lookback within 100 ms latency**, plus no regressions. That isn't
cleanly met:

- L=2 (50 ms) gives F=13.42 on the answered subset — over the 10 Hz target.
- L=10 (250 ms) gives F=10.57 on the answered subset but only answers 50 %
  of female files (the rest are voicing-trapped).
- No lookback within 100 ms gets us under 10 Hz with reasonable null
  recall.

The real choice is among:

1. **Accept (A)'s structural limitation. Ship Stage 2 L=2 as a strict
   improvement over baseline** (13.42 vs 30.65 in this methodology, or
   13.42 vs 14.15 in the prior-baseline single-window methodology — call
   it neutral-to-slightly-better with 50 ms added latency). Acknowledge
   the < 10 Hz target was missed by ~3 Hz and the high null rate is real.
2. **Promote to option (B), voicing-duplicated state space.** Multi-day
   redesign; would address the voicing trap and likely meet the target
   honestly across a much higher fraction of the corpus.
3. **Modify the observation model under (A)** — e.g., make obs[unvoiced]
   based on a different voicing posterior, or add a candidate-mass floor
   so voiced states always carry baseline weight. This is "tuning" by
   your rule, but it's bounded scope (~hours) and might close the gap
   without (B)'s redesign cost. I would NOT do this without your explicit
   OK.
4. **Tune switch_prob** — explicitly disallowed by your prior instruction;
   noting only for completeness.

I lean **(2)**. (A)'s 4× compute saving was justified on the assumption
that the corpus wouldn't expose voicing-continuity issues; that
assumption was wrong. The structural fix is the structural fix; tuning
around it just hides the issue until PTDB-TUG (real-world recordings,
will be even more marginal-CMND than studio Hillenbrand) re-exposes it.

But (1) is also defensible if the 50 % female miss rate is acceptable
when the smoother + voicing fallback in production handle it (the
existing `pitchSmoothing.js` reconciliation is a downstream guard, and
the production trace shows last-known pitch through brief silences).

Awaiting direction. No corpus expansion until this is resolved.
