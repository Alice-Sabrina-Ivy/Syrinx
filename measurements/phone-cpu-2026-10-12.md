# Phone check of the main-thread pass, and the trace redraw cadence — 2026-10-12

Branch `trace-raf60` (from `cue-strip` 1366c39). Public data only: the phone
heard public LibriSpeech test-clean speech played from the PC speakers; the
desktop runs play the LibriSpeech smoke WAVs through Chrome's fake microphone.
Follows [main-thread-cpu-2026-10-08.md](main-thread-cpu-2026-10-08.md), whose
§3.1 left the phone unmeasured and kept the review's display-rate redraw loop
(capped at ~60 Hz) as a phone-check variant.

**Outcome.** On the phone the current `cue-strip` head uses 30 % (panel off) /
34 % (panel on) less main-thread CPU than the base before the main-thread pass.
Its data-driven trace redraw (one draw per 40 ms audio buffer, ~25 draws/s)
scrolls smoothly by the automated screen-recording check but fails guard 3c's
gap bar (p99 52 ms against 50 ms). The variant — an animation-frame loop that
draws at most every 16 ms while data is arriving — draws ~53 times a second
(gap p99 34 ms, guard 3c passes) and also scrolls smoothly, at **+27 % (panel
off) / +30 % (panel on) main-thread CPU** against the head; still 11 % / 14 %
below the base. **User decision (2026-10-12): ship the smoother variant,
accepting that CPU cost.** This branch brings it into
`src/components/traceRedrawScheduler.js` (§4). On the desktop (§5.1, headless
Chrome at ~180 frames/s) the change costs **+63 ms/s (+58 %) panel off and
+61 ms/s (+53 %) panel on** of main thread, and a new point reaches the screen
~6–7 ms later (phone: ~1 ms); trace pixels at data steps and the cue strip are
unchanged.

## 1. Method

### 1.1 Device and harness

- **Phone**: Pixel 11 Pro XL (Tensor G6, Android 17, build
  CD1A.260905.001.B1), Chrome 154.0.8037.127, **portrait**, auto-rotate off,
  Do Not Disturb on, "Stay awake while charging" on, USB-powered. Capture path
  MSTP at 48 kHz; the hardware delivers audio in **40 ms frames** (the desktop
  delivers 25 ms chunks), so the hook appends one or two trace points per
  hardware frame. Thermal status 0 at the start of every run (skin 27.5 °C at
  the first run, 32.9 °C at the last).
- **Harness** (scratch only, not committed): the pattern of
  `scripts/mobile-diag-capture.js` — `adb` + a CDP forward to the phone's
  Chrome tab, the build served by `vite preview` on the PC and reached with
  `adb reverse`. It kills only the PIDs it spawned (preview server, audio
  player, its own `screenrecord`) and removes only its own forward / reverse.
- **Audio**: a public LibriSpeech test-clean loop, 5 women and 5 men in
  alternating blocks of ~30 s, played through the PC speakers next to the
  phone, at the volume of the 2026-10-07 phone session.
- **Run** (each build): direction question answered, Start Listening, 15 s
  warm-up, a **60 s panel-off stage**, the experimental "Likely heard as" panel
  switched on (model ready), 15 s warm-up, a **60 s panel-on stage**, panel off
  again. ~3.5 min per run plus a 30 s rest.
- **CPU figures** (ms of CPU per wall-clock second):
  - **main /proc** — CPU ticks of the renderer's `CrRendererMain` thread from
    the phone's `/proc`;
  - **TaskDuration** — CDP `Performance.getMetrics` TaskDuration of the page's
    main thread;
  - **workers /proc** — the sum over all `DedicatedWorker` threads (Chrome
    names every worker thread the same, so they cannot be split by name);
  - **all Chrome** — every Chrome process on the phone (browser, renderer,
    GPU, utility).
  CPU numbers come from the production page (no `?diag=1`): the diag overlay
  costs ~45–60 ms/s of main thread on the phone. The `?diag=1` runs give the
  trace cadence, latencies, the resonance worker and the gender worker.
- **Women and men**: whole-stage `/proc` CPU covers equal shares of women's and
  men's speech and cannot be split. Every diag metric is split per voice: each
  5 s interval is labelled by its median voiced F0 (≥ 165 Hz women, ≤ 140 Hz
  men) and intervals spanning a block change are dropped.

### 1.3 Runs, the interruption and the cable swap

Planned order: H1, B1, HD (each production / production / diag + screen
recording), then — because HD failed guard 3c — VD and V1. What happened:

1. **H1** completed (13:12–13:15 UTC).
2. **B1** failed at start: `adb devices` returned no device — a flaky USB cable.
3. **HD** (first attempt) started, finished its panel-off stage and screen
   recording, then lost the device during the panel-on stage (13:18). Its
   results were **discarded**: no run file was saved and its recording was not
   analysed.
4. The **cable was replaced** and the batch resumed (13:24) without repeating
   H1 (complete before the fault): **B1**, **HD**, then **VD** and **V1**, all on
   the new cable.

One run per build and page: H1 ran ~12 min before B1 and ~25 min before V1, on
a slightly cooler phone. The CPU differences below are single-run differences
on one device; they are large against the spread seen between repeated desktop
runs of one build (±10 ms/s, §3.1 of the main-thread note), but the phone's
own run-to-run spread was not measured (a second B / H pair was planned only
for the case that HD passed).

### 1.4 Smoothness from a screen recording (automated, rule fixed in advance)

The by-eye judder check was replaced on 2026-10-11 by an automated check whose
rule was written before any capture was analysed (scratch `phone-smooth/RULE.md`;
analyser `analyze.py`). During the panel-off stage of HD and VD,
`adb shell screenrecord` records the phone's real composited screen for 60 s
(1344 × 2992, variable frame rate, presentation timestamps); it is pulled to
the PC and deleted from the phone. The analyser locates the trace plot from the
page's DOM rect and a CDP screenshot (template match on the y-axis labels),
removes the static layer, registers each frame by horizontal sub-pixel shift
and measures the scroll. On `?diag=1` the overlay covers the right of the plot,
so the uncovered left strip is used (the whole trace scrolls as one). A
visible change is ≥ 0.1 CSS px.

**JERKY** if any of: J1 median update interval > 50 ms (< 20 updates/s);
J2 > 3 hitches (pauses > 100 ms) per minute; J3 longest pause > 250 ms;
J4 > 2 % jumps (steps > max(2 × median, 1 CSS px)); J5 > 0.5 % backward steps;
J6 step-size CV > 0.6. INCONCLUSIVE if < 20 s is analysable or the plot is not
found. Amendments after validation touch instrument validity only (J4 / J6 are
assessed only when the capture runs ≥ 1.5 × the update rate and, with a diag
draw record, shows ≥ 95 % of the page's draws; a slow capture can turn a smooth
trace jerky but never the reverse, so only a would-be JERKY result becomes
INCONCLUSIVE). Validated on the desktop before the session: H and V SMOOTH at
25 ms and at 40 ms data cadence; traces held to 8 and 12 Hz and a trace with
150–300 ms stalls every 1.5–3.5 s all JERKY.

- **The cue-strip change cuts the phone's main thread by ~30–34 %** (`/proc`;
  36–40 % by TaskDuration) — less than the desktop's ~62 %. Likely reasons
  (not separated here): the phone's display rate (at most 120 Hz) is below
  headless desktop Chrome's ~152 frames/s, so the old per-frame loop cost
  relatively less; `/proc` counts everything on the thread (capture reader,
  messages, GC) while TaskDuration counts scheduled tasks only; and H vs B
  also contains the row and CPP changes (§1.2).
- **Worker B − H** (−30.4 ms/s panel off, −17.1 panel on) is the DSP worker's
  CPP saving (the only worker changed between B and H), against 17–22 ms/s on
  the desktop ([vocal-weight-pipeline-retire-2026-10-11.md](vocal-weight-pipeline-retire-2026-10-11.md)).
- **V vs H workers** (−27 / −21 ms/s) cannot be the scheduler — no worker code
  differs between H and V. It is the spread of single runs on a phone whose
  cores clock and migrate threads on their own; read worker figures from one
  run as ± ~30 ms/s.
- **The variant's cost**: +68 / +72 ms/s on the main thread and +153 / +184
  ms/s over all Chrome processes (the extra frames are also rastered and
  composited by the GPU process and the compositor thread).

## 4. Decision and change

**Adopted: V** (user decision 2026-10-12) — the smoother trace (52.7 vs 25.4
updates/s, guard 3c passing on the phone), accepting **~+27 % (panel off) /
+30 % (panel on) main-thread CPU on the phone vs H** (+68 / +72 ms/s; +15 %
over all Chrome processes). This reverses §3 F1 of the main-thread note, which
had kept the data-driven cadence to save that per-frame work before the phone
could judge it.

The scheduler in `src/components/traceRedrawScheduler.js` becomes the
variant's logic unchanged (f7032b9's diff from c925457): while data is
arriving (a notification within the last `IDLE_AFTER_MS` = 1 s) an
animation-frame loop draws at most every `MIN_DRAW_MS` = 16 ms (every display
frame at 60 Hz, every other frame at 120 Hz); once data has stopped for 1 s it
draws on a 100 ms timeout (`IDLE_FALLBACK_MS`), as before. Stall safety is
kept and tightened: a feed that stalls while listening still redraws every
display frame, so no freeze is longer than 16 ms plus a frame — inside the
review fix's bound of `FALLBACK_MS` (50 ms) plus a frame, which the unit test
keeps as its guard. A burst of notifications still draws once; a hidden page
keeps at most one pending frame; `stop()` cancels the frame and the timer.

(~110 ms/s) and headless Chrome's ~180 Hz frame rate wakes the loop three
times per draw. On a 60 Hz monitor the loop wakes once per draw; on 120–165 Hz
monitors it is similar to this run.

| | draws/s | gap p99 / max ms | step p99 / max CSS px | draw − readout median | capture → painted median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base, woman / man | 40.0 / 39.9 | 37.5 / 87.7, 38.7 / 83.3 | 1.60 / 3.70, 1.64 / 3.49 | 3.0 / 3.2 ms | 7.6 / 6.2 ms |

Guard 3c passes in all four runs (the > 80 ms maxima in three of them are
machine stalls: the head woman run had 3 main-thread tasks > 50 ms). **New
finding — latency:** on the desktop the trace now shows a new point ~6–7 ms
later than before (draw − readout +5.9 / +7.5 ms, women / men). The data-driven
redraw drew on the first frame after each point; the loop draws only when
≥ 16 ms have passed since its last draw, a phase not tied to the data, so a
point waits on average about half a draw period. On the phone the difference
is ~1 ms (§3: draw − readout H 8.8 / 8.7 → V 9.6 / 10.0 ms, capture → painted
20.0 / 20.4 → 21.4 / 21.6 ms, panel off / on), where the 40 ms buffers already
set the phase. It exceeds the main-thread note's guard 1 bar (+5 ms) on the
desktop; reported, not judged — the decision was made on smoothness. A
possible follow-up (not built): draw on the first frame after a notification
even inside the 16 ms cap.

**Visual check** (`visual-check.mjs` vs 1366c39): **33 / 33 pass** — trace
pixels identical at every data step (24 captures, 0 pixels differ), DPR-only
resize 1 → 2 → 3 → 1 correct in both trees, cue strip identical (3 × 240
ticks). Reported, not judged: the capture 7 ms after a data step is identical
at DPR 1 and differs on 0.8 % of pixels at DPR 3 — most likely because the base's real-time
50 ms fallback timeout (the fixture's clock is manual, its timers are not) can
fire during the slower DPR-3 steps and queue a frame that the +7 ms flush runs;
the head draws nothing within 16 ms of its last draw.

## 6. What remains unmeasured

- **Mid-range and older phones**: one flagship (Tensor G6) only. On a slower
  phone the per-frame cost is a larger share of the main thread, and the
  phone's display rate (60 vs 90 / 120 Hz) sets how often the loop wakes.
- **Landscape** on the phone: this session was portrait (the 2026-10-07
  session was landscape; not comparable).
- **Safari / iOS** and **Firefox** (the AudioContext fallback path, 25 ms
  chunks): not measured; the scheduler only uses `requestAnimationFrame` and
  `setTimeout`.
- **Repeat runs on the phone**: one run per build; the phone's run-to-run
  spread and a second B / H pair are not measured. The split of H vs B into
  the main-thread pass vs the Clarity row + CPP retirement (the optional split
  run) was not run.
- **Battery / thermal over a long session**: 60 s stages only.
