## Why Stage 1 underperforms — analysis

The legacy multi-mult correction at lines 290–380 is a single-frame heuristic
for octave-error suppression: when YIN's first-below-threshold scan locks at
τ = T/k for k ∈ {2, 3, 4} (a sub-harmonic of the true period), the multi-mult
block walks forward to k·τ and switches to that deeper dip. It is a noisy
heuristic — it false-halves ~5 % of female periodic speech (the 27 halved
buckets at baseline) — but it also catches a comparable number of legitimate
2×/3× cases that would otherwise leak through.

`PYIN_STAGE=1` removes that heuristic and replaces it with naive
Beta(2,18)-weighted argmax over candidates. On synthetic stress this is
equivalent or better: those stimuli have a clean depth ratio
(CMND[T] ≪ CMND[T/2]) that lets the deeper-but-larger-τ candidate win the
probability mass. On real-speech 2× cases the depth ratio is shallower —
the strong formant resonance amplifying the 2nd harmonic produces
near-equal CMND at T and T/2 — and Beta-argmax picks the smaller-τ candidate
because Beta(2,18) puts most of its mass on small thresholds.

This is exactly the failure mode the HMM (Stage 2) is designed to fix:
single-frame information cannot reliably distinguish T from T/2 when the
2nd harmonic is comparably periodic, but the temporal-consistency prior
in the Viterbi pass over recent frames CAN — the true track will be
self-consistent across frames in a way the spurious 2× track will not.

## Reading on the prompt's prediction

The prompt said "numbers should be SIMILAR to current behavior, not better"
and warned "Stage 1 WORSE than baseline by more than noise" is a surprise
worth pausing on.

I read this two ways and want your call:

- **(a) Working as intended.** The prediction was based on "Stage 1 ≈ raw
  YIN", and raw-YIN-without-multi-mult IS worse than legacy-with-multi-mult
  on this corpus. The threshold integration itself works (synthetic stress
  passes; PYIN_STAGE=0 path identical to baseline; no parse / index / NaN
  failures). The +9 Hz Stage 1 cost is the multi-mult removal cost, which
  Stage 2's HMM is supposed to recover and surpass. The expected Stage 2
  improvement therefore needs to clear ~+13 Hz of headroom (recover the
  9 Hz lost here AND beat the 14.15 Hz baseline by 4+ Hz to hit < 10 Hz).
  Larger lift than I think we initially scoped, but consistent with paper
  claims of ~3–5× gross-error-rate reduction.

- **(b) Implementation issue I missed.** The 32 new female 2× errors are
  too clean a number to be random — that's a specific count of cases
  where the multi-mult was correctly catching a legitimate halving and
  Stage 1 isn't. But Stage 1 SHOULDN'T be expected to catch those at all;
  that's the HMM's job. So I don't see a bug, but I'd rather you confirm
  before I move on. The synthetic stress passing 5/5 + 4/4 is consistent
  with the algorithm being right.

I lean strongly **(a)**. The error mix is qualitatively the right shape:
multi-mult-style errors disappear, octave-aliasing-without-correction
errors appear in their place. The 2× error rate of 35/576 ≈ 6 % matches
roughly the rate at which YIN's first-below-threshold scan locks at T/2
on female periodic speech, which the multi-mult was previously fixing.
Stage 2's HMM is the right place to put that correction back.
