How to interpret the results
  • Speech F0 range (75–300 Hz): if you see a strong narrow peak here AND you're not speaking, it's likely interference that could confuse pitch detection.
  • Mains hum (50/60/100/120 Hz): electrical interference. A peak around 50 or 60 Hz suggests poorly-shielded equipment or a ground loop.
  • Refrigerator / HVAC (~80–200 Hz): motor / compressor fundamental + harmonics. Try unplugging or moving away from the source.
  • Fan whine (varies): computer / room fans. Depends on RPM and blade count; often 100–250 Hz.
  • Room standing wave: half-wavelength of the peak frequency in cm = 17150 / peak_Hz. If that matches a room dimension, your room is resonating at that frequency.

Prominence is the peak's height above the surrounding noise floor. Peaks with prominence ≥ 10 dB are marked amber; ≥ 18 dB are marked red. The 175 Hz peak that triggers SwiftF0 octave-up errors typically has prominence ≥ 15 dB.
